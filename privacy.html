Privacy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

The short version: everything you put in Lootkit stays on your own computer, in your browser. There are no accounts, no tracking, no ads, and no cookies. We never see your pictures, notes, assets or links.

What Lootkit keeps, and where

  • Your boards, pictures, sticky notes, assets and links are saved in your browser's own storage on your device. They are not sent to us or to anyone else.
  • If you turn on Auto-backup or use Save to computer, Lootkit writes files only into the folder or file you pick. Auto-backup keeps one file called lootkit-backup.json plus one dated copy per day, and removes dated copies older than the newest seven. It does not touch other files.
  • If you clear your browser's data for this site, your work there is deleted. Keep a backup.

Things that do contact other websites

  • Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, so Google receives a normal web request (including your IP address) when the page loads.
  • Quick Links icons are loaded from each linked website (for example fab.com/favicon.ico), so that website receives a normal request.
  • The website host (GitHub Pages) receives a normal request when you open Lootkit and may keep standard server logs. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Links you click open those websites, as any link would.

The Lootkit Saver extension

The optional browser extension adds two items to your right-click menu: Save image to Lootkit board and Save link to my Lootkit links. It also marks assets you already own when you visit an asset store.

  • The right-click items act only when you choose them. Choosing one reads that one picture or link, plus a few words to name it: the link's text or any text you selected, or the picture's description (or, if it has none, the page's title). It downloads the picture from its website, the same way the page itself loads it (with that website's normal cookies), so it can hand it to Lootkit. It does not keep the address of the page you were on.
  • Saved items wait in the extension's own storage on your computer until Lootkit is open, then they move into Lootkit and are removed from the extension.
  • On web pages it highlights the link you right-click, and shows a small "Saved" note. Apart from the asset-store check described below, it does not read, collect or send anything else from the pages you visit.
  • It hands items only to the Lootkit page itself. Nothing is sent to us or to any other server.
  • "You own this" on asset stores: Lootkit gives the extension a copy of your asset list (each asset's name, store link, store, price, purchase date and projects — no screenshots). The extension keeps this copy on your computer and, only on Fab, the Unity Asset Store, itch.io, Gumroad, Sketchfab and CGTrader, compares it with the page you are looking at to show a "You own this" note. It only reads those pages; it never clicks, buys or sends anything. Removing the extension removes the copy.
  • Why it asks for access to all websites: so the right-click menu works on any site and it can download the picture you chose.

Children

Lootkit does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
